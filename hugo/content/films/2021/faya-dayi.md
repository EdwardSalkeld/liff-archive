In the hypnotic and hazily dreamlike documentary Faya Dayi, shot
in gorgeous, silvery black and white tones, filmmaker Jessica Beshir
plunges the viewer into a spiritual journey and an anthropological
exploration of the rituals of ‘khat’. Khat is a leaf chewed by Sufi Muslims
for religious meditations that creates a mild euphoria in its users and
has become the single most lucrative cash crop in Ethiopia today. The
film follows teenaged protagonist Mohammed and his friends as they
witness the seductive and destructive effects on the adults around them.
